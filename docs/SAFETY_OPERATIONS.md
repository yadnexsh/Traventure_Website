# Safety Operations

**Status:** Operational framework baseline.

## 1. Purpose and limits

The platform supports safety information, readiness checks, communications, and recordkeeping. It cannot guarantee safety or replace trained trek leaders, qualified medical advice, local knowledge, emergency services, or operational judgement.

## 2. Trek-specific safety content

For every active trek, require reviewed information appropriate to the route:
- Fitness/experience prerequisites and any restrictions.
- Altitude and acclimatization considerations.
- Terrain, weather variability, and known hazards.
- Equipment and packing guidance.
- Emergency and evacuation arrangements verified for that operating area.
- Source, reviewer, and last-reviewed date.

Do not claim guaranteed weather, rescue capability, communications coverage, or risk-free participation.

## 3. Capacity and Operational Limits

- Total departure capacity reflects the absolute physical and operational limit of the trek.
- A commercial decision to add more seats must **never** override the trek's approved operational or safety limit.
- Any discrepancy in capacity must be corrected by an authorized admin. Capacity increases must be validated against safety guidelines and logged in the audit trail.
- Do not permit total capacity to be reduced below the number of seats already committed or otherwise allocated. The UI must clearly explain invalid capacity changes.

## 4. Participant Information and Access

- **Data Minimization:** Collect only the health and emergency contact information strictly required for a documented safety or operational purpose. Avoid expansive medical histories.
- Explain the purpose and access policy before collection.
- Do not infer medical fitness from a questionnaire or automated score.
- **Restricted Access:** Trek leaders should see only information needed for assigned departures and only for the necessary time. Emergency contacts and declarations are restricted to authorized personnel.
- Never expose sensitive data in public pages, URLs, ordinary logs, analytics, or unprotected email.

## 5. Weather, Route, and Safety Notices

A notice should include:
- Verified message and source.
- Timestamp and timezone.
- Affected trek/departures.
- Publication and expiry/review time.

## 6. Incident records

Restrict incident/near-miss reports to designated roles. Capture only necessary information, keep an audit trail, and define retention and review. Do not use incident narratives as general admin notes.

## 7. Review checklist before using live

- [ ] Qualified safety owner assigned.
- [ ] Trek-specific hazards and prerequisites reviewed.
- [ ] Participant data access, minimization, and retention approved.
- [ ] Weather/route notice workflow tested.
- [ ] Incident escalation workflow defined.
- [ ] No placeholder or unverified safety content remains.
